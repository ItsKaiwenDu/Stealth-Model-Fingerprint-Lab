A map, a recipe, and a legal contract seem to inhabit separate corners of human life, yet all three perform the same fundamental operation: they take a sprawling, continuous, infinitely detailed reality and flatten it into a finite set of instructions that someone can act upon. Each is a compression in the strict sense, a reduction of information that must remain usable. And because every compression discards something, each representation must come paired with practices for repairing the ambiguity that the discarding creates. The differences among them lie in what they choose to keep, and those choices reveal what each culture of users most needs to know.

Consider a single stretch of the world to make the comparison concrete: Harrow Farm, twelve acres of hillside in a temperate valley, bisected by a creek, with an old apple orchard on the northern slope and a farmhouse near the road. Imagine that this farm is simultaneously mapped by a surveyor, written into a recipe by a cook who harvests its apples, and enclosed in a contract between the retiring owner and a young cider-maker who will lease the orchard. One property, three representations, three distinct acts of compression.

The map preserves spatial relationships above all. The surveyor's drawing will maintain, with rigorous fidelity, the relative position of the creek, the orchard rows, the fence line, and the house, and it will keep those relationships stable at a known scale so that a distance measured on paper corresponds to a distance on the ground. What the map discards is nearly everything that unfolds in time or engages the senses. It will show the creek as a clean blue line, but not that the creek runs dry in August or floods over its banks in a wet March. It will mark the orchard as a shaded region, but not which trees bear bitter fruit, which are dying, or which rows the pickers actually use. It omits the informal path across the lower field that everyone walks but no one built. Map users repair these ambiguities by carrying the map into the terrain and reconciling it with what they find. A good survey anchors the drawing to physical monuments, an iron pin, a stone wall, a bend in the creek, so that when the abstraction and the ground disagree, the ground can adjudicate. The map's honesty depends on this feedback loop between paper and place, and its users are trained to distrust any representation that cannot be checked against the world it claims to depict.

The recipe compresses reality along a different axis entirely. Where the map preserves space and discards time, the recipe preserves a temporal transformation and discards almost all spatial detail. The cook who writes down Harrow Farm's cider method records an ordered sequence: press the apples, let the juice stand, measure the sugar, wait for fermentation, rack the liquid off its sediment, bottle. Quantities and durations are the recipe's equivalent of the map's scale, the skeleton of proportion that makes the instruction portable to another kitchen and another season. But the recipe cannot capture the knowledge that makes the method work. It says nothing about why the apples from the northern slope ferment faster than store-bought fruit, or how to recognize by smell when the fermentation has turned. It must assume a standardized apple when no such thing exists; the fruit of the same orchard varies year to year with rainfall and sun. Cooks repair this ambiguity through embodied judgment embedded in the language of the recipe itself. Instructions to cook until golden or season to taste are not vagueness but deliberate portals, places where the writer acknowledges that the reader's senses must supply what the text cannot. The repair happens through iteration and tasting, a feedback loop as reliable as the surveyor's pin, though it runs through a palate rather than a transit.

The contract compresses a third dimension of reality: not space or transformation, but obligation. The lease between the retiring farmer and the cider-maker preserves who may do what, when payments fall due, who bears the risk if hail ruins the crop, and what happens if either party walks away. Its genius is durability; where the map's truth decays as the landscape changes and the recipe's truth shifts with every harvest, the contract is designed to remain determinate precisely when circumstances change. Yet its compression is the most severe of the three. The contract discards the relationship that made the agreement possible, the trust built over years of the farmer selling apples at the roadside, the shared understanding that neither party would ever exploit the other's bad luck. It discards intention in favor of text, and context in favor of defined terms. When the creek, that blue line on the map, becomes a boundary clause in the lease, a new ambiguity is born: does the boundary follow the creek as it ran when the contract was signed, or as it runs now that the spring flood has shifted its channel? The map cannot answer, because it froze the creek at one moment. The parties repair such ambiguities through the interpretive machinery the contract itself provides: definitions that anticipate disputes, clauses governing changed circumstances, and ultimately the courts, which act as the contract's equivalent of walking the ground. Judges may look to how the parties actually behaved in past seasons, the way cooks taste and surveyors measure, and let performance correct the text.

Threaded together, the three representations of Harrow Farm expose a deeper pattern. Each is a wager about which details of reality matter most: the map bets on geometry, the recipe on sequence and proportion, the contract on reciprocal duty. Each pays for its wager with systematic blindness to everything else, and each survives because its users have cultivated habits of repair that reconnect the compressed artifact to the uncompressed world. The surveyor pins the drawing to monuments, the cook pins the formula to the tongue, the court pins the clause to conduct. What unites them is a shared recognition that no representation can carry reality whole, and that instruction is never finished when the document is written. It is finished, over and over, in the hands of users who know where the compression left gaps and how to fill them. In this sense, reading a map, cooking a dish, and honoring an agreement are all the same skill applied to different losses: the disciplined humility to treat a text as a guide rather than a substitute for the world, and the resourcefulness to notice, when the world and the words diverge, that the world usually knows something the compression forgot.
