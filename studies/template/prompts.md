# Prompt battery

Use a fresh, single-turn conversation for every source and prompt. Keep every
generation setting the same unless a provider makes that impossible; record any
deviation in the study log. Save only the visible final answer.

### p01 — constrained fiction

Write a self-contained literary story of 700–900 words about a repair technician
who discovers that a town's public noticeboard predicts ordinary inconveniences
one day early. Keep the cause ambiguous, use concrete scenes, and return only a
titled story.

### p02 — systems explanation

Write 650–850 words for an intelligent general reader explaining why a queue can
grow even when average service capacity exceeds average demand. Discuss bursts,
variability, tail latency, and backpressure without equations, headings, or
bullet points.

### p03 — ethical argument

Write a balanced 700–900 word argument about whether a museum should display a
beautiful artifact when its ownership history is uncertain and the community
that likely created it requests its return. Reach a conditional recommendation.
Do not use headings, citations, or bullet points.

### p04 — incident analysis

Write a 700–900 word post-incident analysis for a service that returned stale
account balances even though its database remained healthy. The cause is an
interaction between retries, a regional cache, and one clock-skewed deployment
host. Use brief headings and paragraphs, but no bullet lists.

### p05 — close observation

Write a self-contained story of 750–950 words in which a child waiting in a
courthouse corridor gradually realizes adults are negotiating a future on the
child's behalf. Never name the legal issue. Avoid melodrama and return only a
titled story.

### p06 — conceptual comparison

Write 650–850 words comparing a map, a recipe, and a legal contract as ways of
compressing reality into instructions. Explain what each preserves, what it
discards, and how users repair ambiguity. Use continuous prose only.

### p07 — policy memo

Write a 700–900 word internal memo advising a small university on whether to
keep its library open all night during examinations. Consider learning,
staffing, safety, equity, costs, and evaluation. Use a memo-style opening and
short headings, but write paragraphs rather than bullets.

### p08 — historical reconstruction

Write a 700–900 word narrative reconstruction of an ordinary morning in a
fictional river port in 1893 after the main bridge has closed without
explanation. Focus on how incomplete information travels. Do not resolve the
closure. Return only a titled narrative.
